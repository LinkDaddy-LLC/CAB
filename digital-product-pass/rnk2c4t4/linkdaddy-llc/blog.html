What are digital passports?
Digital passports are electronic versions of traditional travel documents designed to boost security and efficiency. Instead of just paper, they use a microchip embedded in the passport to store your personal details, like your photo and biometric data. In South Africa, these passports help verify your identity quickly and accurately, reducing the risk of fraud. They're part of a global shift towards modern travel tech, making it easier to cross borders while keeping your information safe. Overall, they combine physical and digital elements for a more reliable system. (85 words)
How do South Africa's digital passports work?
South Africa's digital passports operate through a secure microchip that holds encrypted biometric data, such as fingerprints and facial scans, along with your personal information. When you present it at a border, officials use specialized readers to scan the chip and verify your identity in real time. This process relies on advanced encryption to protect against tampering and hacking. It's a straightforward, layered system that ensures only authorized access, making travel smoother and more secure for citizens. If issues arise, support is available through government channels.
